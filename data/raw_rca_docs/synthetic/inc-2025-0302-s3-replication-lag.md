## Summary

A nightly catalog-ingestion batch job wrote 2.3 million updated product records (18.4 TB) into the `prod-catalog-us-east-1` S3 bucket in a single 22-minute burst, overwhelming cross-region replication (CRR) to `prod-catalog-us-west-2-replica`. Replication lag, normally under 60 seconds, climbed to a peak of 4 hours 12 minutes (15,120 seconds) once AWS KMS began throttling the decrypt/re-encrypt calls CRR issues for SSE-KMS objects. CloudFront's regional read origin in `us-west-2` kept serving the last-replicated version of catalog objects, so west-coast traffic saw stale prices and stock levels for up to four hours, and 2.4% of checkout attempts failed with a price-mismatch error. Resolved by an emergency KMS quota increase and, longer-term, enabling S3 Replication Time Control (RTC).

## Root Cause

S3 CRR for SSE-KMS-encrypted objects issues a `kms:Decrypt` call against the source key and a `kms:Encrypt` call against the destination key for every replicated object. The account's default KMS request quota (5,500 requests/second) was sized for steady-state traffic and could not absorb the burst generated by `catalog-etl-nightly` writing its full daily payload in a 22-minute window rather than throttled over time. That burst is the trigger; the KMS quota ceiling is the root cause of the replication backlog. The factor that turned a backend delay into a customer-facing incident was that the CloudFront failover origin in `us-west-2` treated the replica bucket as authoritative regardless of `ReplicationLatency`, serving stale objects with normal cache-control headers instead of falling back to the source region.

## Impact

Roughly 41,000 catalog page views and 6,200 checkout attempts in `us-west-2` were served data up to 4h12m stale between 02:47 and 11:26 UTC. 2.4% of checkout attempts (149 orders) failed price-mismatch validation and were abandoned; no order was charged an incorrect price. Estimated impact: $9,800 in abandoned-cart value and $3,400 issued in goodwill customer credits. No data loss occurred.

## Detection

Detected by CloudWatch alarm `S3-CRR-ReplicationLatency-High` on the `ReplicationLatency` metric, threshold `> 900` seconds for 2 consecutive 5-minute periods. Time from the first KMS throttling event (02:47 UTC) to alarm firing (09:14 UTC) was 6 hours 27 minutes because the alarm evaluated only a trailing window and did not detect a slowly-climbing, never-recovering lag until it crossed the absolute threshold.

## Resolution

1. Opened an urgent AWS Support case for a KMS request-rate quota increase on the source and destination keys.
2. Paused the next scheduled `catalog-etl-nightly` run to stop the backlog from growing further.
3. Monitored `ReplicationLatency` until it returned to baseline after the quota increase.
4. Invalidated CloudFront's cache for the 2.3M affected object keys to force fresh reads once replication caught up.

## Action Items

- Enable S3 Replication Time Control (RTC) on `prod-catalog-us-east-1` to guarantee a 15-minute replication SLA with built-in backpressure alerting. Owner: storage-platform-team, JIRA-4821.
- Rate-limit `catalog-etl-nightly` to batches of at most 200K objects per 10-minute window instead of one burst. Owner: catalog-platform-team, JIRA-4822.
- Add a staleness check so `us-west-2` reads fall back to `us-east-1` directly when `ReplicationLatency` exceeds 300 seconds. Owner: edge-platform-team, JIRA-4823.
- Lower the `S3-CRR-ReplicationLatency-High` threshold to 300 seconds and add a rate-of-change alarm. Owner: observability-team, JIRA-4824.
